# A Collar Is a Contract

Miso lets me examine the collar after everyone else has left the kitchen. That seems important. He waits until Leila is carrying the mugs away, then jumps onto the table and sits with his head turned so the brass plate faces me.

I don't touch it immediately.

“What happens if I ask the wrong thing?”

“Usually I can't answer. Sometimes I can, and regret it.”

“Who put it on?”

“Adrian.”

“What does it do?”

“Keeps me available.”

I look towards the manual on the table.

“The same system.”

“Older version. He practised.”

Miso says it flatly. He has stopped doing anything with his tail.

The brass plate is scratched almost smooth. Under the scratches, a name has been pressed into the metal. I can read the first mark, but the rest seem to turn away when I look directly at them.

“Is that your name?”

“A name I gave him. I was looking for somewhere warm. He had a house with a boiler room. He asked if I would help keep the doors in order.”

“What kind of doors?”

“Ordinary ones, then less ordinary ones. I should have asked better.”

“You're a cat.”

“Yes.”

I wait for a joke. He doesn't provide one.

“Are you Bast?”

“No.” His ears flatten. “She has more important complaints.”

“Then what are you?”

“A guardian. Small place. Small work. Places collect habits. Some of us learn how to hold them.”

“The flat?”

“Not originally.”

He looks at the window. A pigeon lands outside, inspects the sill and leaves. His head follows it before he remembers to be upset.

“Why did you take Mum to him?”

“I knew she could help with the doors. I didn't know he meant to close one behind her.”

I want him to say he fought, that he tried to warn her, that he spent eleven months planning to fix it. I don't want a simple statement that leaves me to work out what to feel.

“Did you try to stop him afterwards?”

“Yes.”

“How?”

He turns so I can see the bare strip beneath the collar. There are healed scratches on his skin, close together.

I sit back.

“I need to know where his workshop is,” I say.

“Then ask me where the equipment was stored when I last saw it. Don't ask where he is now. The wording matters.”

I ask carefully. He gives me an address near the waterfront, then specifies a side entrance and a set of internal stairs. Julian writes it down from the doorway. Neither of us had heard him return.

Miso watches the pen move. “Second floor. Room with no windows. There was a yellow cupboard.”

“What opened it?” Julian asks.

The collar tightens. I see Miso's shoulders rise before he can stop them.

“Stop,” I say.

Julian puts the pen down. “Sorry.”

I look at the release diagram. Leila has made me repeat it until I can draw it without looking. The collar is a connection. It carries an agreement. If I can hold both ends and allow a refusal—

Miso steps back as my hand moves.

“Ask.”

I stop.

“Can I try to loosen it?”

He looks at the chalk. Then at me.

“Not off. Not yet. If it breaks, he knows where I am. Loosen it enough that I can choose where to stand.”

I draw on the table around his paws. The marks need a definite subject. I say his name, Miso, and feel the circle lose its shape.

“That's what we call you,” I say. “It doesn't work.”

“It works for me.”

I try again. This time I think about the cat sitting in front of me, the yoghurt lid, the cold fire escape, his decision to come when my door opened. The collar is attached to a name Voss has kept. I am trying to make room for a name its wearer still uses.

The chalk lifts a fraction from the table.

Miso closes his eyes. He breathes out. The collar loosens enough to leave a narrow gap.

My hands cramp. Julian puts a glass of water within reach but doesn't touch me. I hold the line for twelve seconds, then twenty. At thirty, Miso steps out of the circle.

The collar stays on. It is visibly looser.

He sits on the far end of the table, trying to look as if nothing has happened. I drink the water with both hands.

“Did it help?” I ask.

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Leila comes back and sees the chalk marks. She begins to speak, then looks at Miso and closes her mouth.

I clean the table before she can ask. Miso follows the cloth with one paw, a movement so ordinary I almost miss it.

At the kitchen door he stops.

“The yellow cupboard has a false back,” he tells Julian. “He changed the lock. Not the wall.”

Then he goes to sit on my clean school shirt.
