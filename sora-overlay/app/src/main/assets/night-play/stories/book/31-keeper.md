# Sunday Service

I begin by trying to answer every light. Within an hour, I understand why Mum made lists.

Some people want a message carried. Some want to know why they were called. Some have been waiting so long they are angry before I speak. I tell each one what the line can do. I don't promise a receiver will answer.

Miso finds a cupboard with a dry blanket inside and carries it to the foot of my chair. He spends a long time arranging it, then lies down on the bare floor beside it.

“You could use that,” I tell him.

“So could you.”

I pull it over my knees. The station is cold when nobody is moving through it.

At noon, the second reader opens a controlled line from the annex. Julian is on the other end. I can hear him testing the connection by reading out the time, then a list of objects on his desk.

“Can you hear all that?”

“Yes.”

“Good. Don't answer when it's just the test tone.”

“I have learned one or two things.”

“I'm writing them down anyway.”

Mum comes on after him. She is in hospital. I can hear the soft bleep of a monitor and Leila talking to someone farther away.

“Have you eaten?” she asks.

I look around the station. I'm not hungry. I don't know whether that is reassuring.

“I need to find out how that works here.”

“I've got the manuals.”

“Of course you have.”

For a while neither of us says anything else. I listen to the room around her. A trolley passes. Someone laughs. A plastic curtain slides along a rail. I would never have chosen a hospital as a place to miss so badly.

“{{firstName}},” she says at last. “I shouldn't have needed you to do this.”

“I know.”

“You can be angry about it.”

“I am.”

She breathes out. I am glad I said it before it became one more thing neither of us could ask.

Wren comes on briefly. She tells me that snow melts at different speeds depending on what I put it on. She has been conducting tests on a windowsill, a glove and the sleeve of Julian's cardigan. He has objected to the cardigan.

Imani is last.

“The chair,” she says.

“Still terrible.”

“Good. Means we're talking to the right person.”

I laugh for the first time since the stairs closed. Miso raises his head to check the sound, then puts it down again.

After the call ends, I find a new line waiting on the board. It is not connected to a person. It has my number written beside it, in handwriting I recognise as my own.

The destination is Friday, 02:13.

I examine the routing stamp. One day after arrival, not the day of departure. The date my phone showed. Nadia's manual calls it a reserved receiving slot.

I've spent two days wondering who was on the other end. Now the empty socket waits for a voice.
