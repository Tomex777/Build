# The Voices Apart

“Clear it,” I tell Voss. “Then leave the switch.”

He doesn't move.

Wren turns towards him. For a moment she is Eva, exactly as she stood in the photograph. I see Voss's whole body lean forward.

Then Wren returns to her own face.

“I could keep doing that,” she says. “Would you ever stop asking?”

He looks at the photograph. At the master panel. At Nadia, trying not to cry out while another cable pulls against her hand.

At last he opens the cache switch.

The photograph tears across its folded crease. He catches both halves and holds them together, although the picture no longer lines up.

The count on the reader drops.

“Imani,” I say.

She moves to Julian. Together they hold the brass box level. Voss stays where Dennis's voice through the phone tells him to stay. It is an arrangement nobody would have believed possible two days ago.

I open the release side of the mark.

The first voice is a man's. He asks whether his brother collected the tools from the shed. It is so ordinary I almost laugh. Wren looks at the label turning towards her, then at the receiving indicator. A light flashes. The man says thank you, impatiently, as if everyone has taken too long about a simple errand.

His label falls flat.

A second voice follows. Then four. Then enough that I can't separate the words. Wren can. I can see the work in the way she holds her shoulders, the careful pauses between one acknowledgement and the next.

Mum's hand begins to open.

Leila reaches her first. She doesn't pull. She slides a clean piece of cloth under the burned palm and supports the wrist while the cable loosens.

Nadia looks at her sister.

“I'm sorry.”

“Later,” Leila says. Her voice is shaking. “Do that later.”

Mum laughs once and starts crying. The brass loop opens. Her hand comes free.

For a second I nearly let go of the chalk.

Imani says my name sharply. I breathe and hold the mark.

Wren is fading at the edges. Her card remains solid on the table, a small plain rectangle amid the moving light.

“Where do you want to stand?” I ask her.

She looks at the doorway. At the strip of lake visible beyond it. At Miso's paw beside my chalk circle.

“Here. For now.”

“Then keep that.”

She presses one hand to the card. Her face steadies.

A woman speaks through the reader. I recognise Mrs. Bell's voice before the name turns towards me. She does not ask about a pharmacy this time.

“Martin,” she says.

The letter in my notebook grows warm. Somewhere far above me, a receiving line opens for one message. I don't hear the rest. It isn't addressed to me.

Miso lifts his paw. I hold the connection alone for the last few seconds.

The final voice is very quiet. A girl says, “You can put it down now.”

Voss closes his eyes. He doesn't answer. He doesn't reach for a switch. He folds the two halves of the photograph together and lowers his hands.

Julian releases the lever.

Nothing rings.

The labels settle. The cables hang loose. The weight at the far end of my mark lifts so suddenly that I fall onto one knee.

Imani catches the chalk before it rolls under the table.

“Done?” she asks Julian.

He checks the indicator, then checks it again because he doesn't trust a first answer anymore.

“Done.”

Wren stands beside the reader with her hands on the table. She is still there. Her coat is too big. One glove is missing. Her face is hers.

Mum takes a step towards me and almost falls. Leila catches her under one arm. I take the other side, feeling the weight of her ordinary body for the first time since she disappeared.

Miso heads for the stairs without waiting to be told. Wren picks her card up carefully and follows him. Julian carries the reader with both hands; his grey fingers have begun to take colour around the knuckles.

Voss remains beside the master panel.

“Adrian,” Leila says.

He looks up.

“You're coming out too.”

He nods. Picks up the empty equipment bag. Follows.

At the staircase, Mum stops to look back once. I let her. Then I put my hand over hers and help her towards the light.
