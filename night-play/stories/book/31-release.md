# What Comes Back With Me

At the top of the stairs, Dennis gives Mum a blanket before he asks her name. She holds it around her shoulders with the good hand and looks at the ordinary wall behind her as if she can't believe it has stopped being a doorway.

Leila phones for an ambulance. This part she knows how to do. She gives the location, the injuries, the number of people who need assessment. She does not start with the impossible explanation.

Voss sits on a low concrete step. When the police arrive, he gets up without being asked. Leila tells them he has information about Nadia's disappearance and unsafe equipment in two buildings. There will be time later to work out which words can fit in a statement. For now, she gives names and addresses and makes sure they are written down.

Imani phones her dad.

I hear her say, “I'm safe,” then, “I know,” then, “I can't explain all of it over the phone.” She keeps the call going until he reaches her. When he arrives, he hugs her so tightly her feet move off the ground.

Mum watches them. I stand beside her, close enough that my sleeve touches the blanket.

“You can do that,” I say.

She looks at me, uncertain for the first time in a way that has nothing to do with the machinery.

I move first. She wraps the blanket around both of us. Her shoulder is thinner than I remember. I press my face into it and finally stop keeping track of who is watching.

At the hospital, the receptionist asks whether Nadia has identification. Leila produces a folder. Of course she brought a folder. I begin laughing and can't stop until it turns into something else.

Wren waits with Imani in a row of plastic chairs. A nurse asks whether she is family. Wren looks at the card in her hand, then at me.

“Yes,” I say, and then stop before deciding for her. “If she wants.”

Wren thinks about it. “I can wait.”

The nurse gives her a visitor sticker. Wren spends the next hour peeling one corner up and pressing it down again, fascinated by how it stays when she chooses not to hold it.

Julian's hand has regained most of its colour. He still can't bend two fingers properly. Leila makes sure somebody examines them rather than accepting that magical damage must require magical neglect.

Miso stays outside the hospital doors. I find him under a bench when I come out for air. His bare neck looks strangely small without the collar.

“Where will you go?” I ask.

He looks at me. Then at the hospital entrance.

“Where's the clean shirt?”

I sit on the bench. The sun has risen. I missed the moment it happened.

My phone is full of ordinary notifications: school messages, a discount code, somebody replying to a group chat that has carried on without knowing anything about my weekend. I read each one because I can.

Under them is a new draft call. No number. No caller ID.

The destination is Friday, 02:13.

I don't press it yet. I take the phone inside, where Mum can see it too.
