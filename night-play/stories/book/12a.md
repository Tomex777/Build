# The Water in the Stairwell

The return address does not open on the first attempt. Julian checks your copied marks against the witness receipt, then against the number on Voss's photograph.

“He's changed the route again.”

“Can he stop us?”

“He can make the line take a different way.”

Miso sniffs the bottom of the wall. Water has begun to seep through a crack in the skirting board. It pools around his paws and reflects a ceiling the exchange room doesn't have.

“Back,” he says.

Julian grabs the equipment box. Imani takes your sleeve. The water rises in a sheet, climbing away from the floor until it stands between you and the booths.

On the other side, the reflected ceiling becomes a long throat.

You make a sound you won't later describe as a scream. Imani pulls you behind the desk as the water collapses where you were standing. It strikes the floor hard enough to split a tile.

Miso is already on a shelf. His ears are flat. Julian has the notebook open with one hand and is trying to protect the paper with the other.

“What is that?” you ask.

“A bad route,” he says. “Very bad.”

A shape gathers inside the water, narrow at the front, thickening along the floor. It moves like a snake, except the surface keeps showing things behind you. Your kitchen. A hospital corridor. The underside of Mum's table.

You remember pictures of the great serpent opposing the sun's nightly passage. The thing on the floor is much smaller, assembled out of borrowed reflections and a bad instruction. That does not make its approach feel manageable.

Julian finds a note in Mum's manual.

“It's following the strongest return signal.”

All three of you look at your phone.

You turn it off. The water keeps coming.

“Not the power,” Julian says. “The claim.”

The blue notebook is warm under your arm. You take it out and the leading edge turns towards it.

Mum's address. Your connection to her. The thing isn't choosing you because you're important. It is taking the shortest available route.

You put the notebook on the desk and step away. The water follows it, gathering against the wooden legs.

Imani looks at the high shelf near the wall. “Can it climb?”

As if answering, the water begins to climb.

Julian passes you chalk. “Hold the connection between it and the address. Don't try to hold all the water.”

You kneel behind the desk. The floor is wet. The first line dissolves before you finish it.

“Paper,” you say.

Imani tears a page from the back of an old catalogue and puts it on a dry part of the desk. You draw the mark there. It is harder with your hands shaking. You close the circle and give it a definite subject: route to address, not address to person.

The rising water pauses.

Miso jumps to the top of the wall, where a pipe passes through a hole larger than it needs to be. He scratches at the plaster. On the other side, a narrow staircase appears, leading down past the telephone booths.

“Empty channel,” he says. “Can you send it there?”

“Can it hurt someone?” you ask.

“Not if we close it before the next arrival.”

That is not a completely reassuring answer. You think of the boy with the bread and the platforms where people wait. You don't want to push a danger out of sight and call it solved.

Julian studies the address on the notebook.

“We can use an unassigned return. No receiver. Then close both ends.”

“How?”

He shows you the open stroke on the temporary hold, then turns the page so you can see how the route should finish. You have to move the mark, not break it. If you erase the wrong side, the water will return to the strongest signal. You.

Imani puts the notebook inside the equipment box and slides the box towards the staircase. The water turns. You move the chalk line a little at a time, opening one end only after the next has been drawn.

Your hand cramps. The water trembles. You tell Imani to stop moving and wait until you can feel your fingers again.

She waits. It matters more than any encouragement she could have shouted.

At the staircase, the route narrows to a stream. The rest of the room drains into it with a sound like a bathtub emptying through a wall.

Julian counts down from three. You close the far end. He closes the near one.

The water vanishes.

For a moment nobody moves. Then the desk collapses, one leg giving way where the first impact cracked it. The notebook slides out of the box and lands at your feet, dry except along one corner.

Imani sits down on a chair that has survived and bends over her knees. Julian wipes the floor with a towel so he can examine the broken route without stepping in it. Miso climbs down from the wall, misses the last shelf and pretends he meant to land that loudly.

You pick up the notebook.

In the margin beside the return address, Mum has written a small instruction you hadn't understood before: Do not confuse connection with appetite.

You add your own note underneath.

Wet floors. Bring paper.

It isn't elegant. You think she will know what you mean.

The green door appears in the wall where you first tried the address. Through its wire-glass window, a woman sits at a table.

You stand up so fast the chair behind you falls over.
