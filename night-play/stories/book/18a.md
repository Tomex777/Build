# Mara's Door

I find Eva's mother's address in a letter attached to the old equipment inventory. The letter asks the archive to stop forwarding calls to her number. It is dated three weeks after Eva died.

Leila reads it without saying anything for a long time.

“I didn't know about this.”

“Would it have changed anything?” I ask.

She looks at me. “It should have.”

I go because Julian needs to establish which call was the first, and because the archive record lists Mara Voss as a receiving witness. I do not go because a grieving woman owes me an explanation. I repeat that distinction to myself on the pavement outside her building, trying to make sure it is more than a sentence that lets me knock.

Imani comes with me. Julian waits in a café across the road with the equipment. Miso has refused to leave the annex's radiator, where he is recovering from the loosened collar.

Mara answers through the intercom. I give Nadia's name and say there may be a record she can correct. The silence lasts long enough that I start preparing to leave.

Then the door buzzes.

Her flat has plants on the windowsill and a bicycle leaning against the hall wall. It is not arranged around Eva's absence in a way I can read. I am ashamed of having looked for that.

She lets me stand inside the doorway but does not offer chairs.

“Adrian sent you?”

“No.”

“Does he know you're here?”

“No.”

She studies me. There is a family resemblance I didn't notice in the photograph: Eva's eyebrows, the way she held one shoulder slightly higher than the other.

I show her the inventory letter. Mara reads it and hands it back.

“I asked them to disconnect it.”

“They didn't.”

“I worked that out.”

I start to explain what the line has become. She holds up one hand.

“I don't want a demonstration.”

“We haven't brought one.”

“Good.”

Imani gives her the copied witness entry. It is a better choice than offering another story about the dead. Mara checks the date.

“That wasn't when she called.”

She goes to a drawer and brings back a calendar. Not the one Voss kept. This one has appointments, bills, ordinary things continuing after the funeral. She finds a note written between a dentist appointment and a reminder to return a library book.

“Here. I wrote it down because I thought I was losing my mind.”

The date is earlier than the archive entry by four days.

Julian's notes come back to me. First contact treated as continuing authority. Reopened channels counted as the same call. Voss changed not just the destination but the point where the record began.

“Can we copy that date?”

Mara nods. I write only the date and the source of the correction. When I hand the calendar back, she looks relieved enough that I understand she expected me to ask for the whole page.

“Did she say anything else?” I ask, then stop. “You don't have to tell us.”

Mara leans against the wall.

“She told me where she put a note. That's all I needed.”

I wait.

“Adrian wanted another conversation. Then a longer one. Then proof the next voice was her. There was always something else the call needed to do before he could let it end.”

She doesn't say whether she forgave him. I don't ask.

“My mum's trapped in his system,” I say.

Mara looks towards the window. On the sill, a plant has grown against the glass and bent back towards the room.

“I know she's missing.”

“We found her.”

Her attention returns to me at once.

“Alive?”

“Yes.”

She closes her eyes. Not in disbelief. As if she has been holding a fact in one place and must now move it without dropping everything around it.

“Tell her I'm sorry I didn't call sooner.”

“You didn't do this.”

Mara opens her eyes.

“You don't know what I did or didn't do.”

The correction is fair. I had offered her the comfortable sentence because it was easier than hearing another difficult account.

I nod. “I'll tell her.”

At the door, Mara asks for Leila's number. I give it only after calling my aunt to ask. The small delay feels awkward. Mara waits without objection.

Outside, Imani walks half a block before speaking.

“You nearly said it.”

“What?”

“That she'd helped us, so now she had to help again.”

“I didn't.”

“No. You stopped.”

Across the road, Julian has ordered one tea and been given a pot large enough for four people. He has spent the waiting time drawing the receiving circuit on a napkin, which the server has tactfully avoided clearing.

I show him the corrected date. He compares it with the two inventory references, then draws a line beneath the original call.

“This is the one. Eva's voice, not a later imitation.”

Imani pours the remaining tea into two cups. It is almost cold. I drink it anyway.

Through the café window, Mara cycles past wearing a bright green helmet. She doesn't see me. She is going somewhere that has nothing to do with my investigation.

I watch her turn the corner, then look back at the number Julian has circled.
