# Things You Can Bandage

Leila doesn't ask whether you found Nadia. She sees you carrying Julian and opens the fire door wider.

You sit him in a chair. His hand is grey to the wrist, the fingers stiff and curled. Imani puts the broken hockey stick against the wall. The blade has separated along one layer, leaving a thin splinter that she keeps touching with her thumb.

Leila cuts Julian's sleeve away.

“Can you feel that?”

“Yes.”

“This?”

“No.”

She asks the same questions twice more, then turns to Miso.

“What travelled up the connection?”

“Claim. Not damage. Yet.”

“Can you reverse it?”

“Not while it has his agreement.”

Julian looks up. “I didn't agree.”

Miso's tail moves sharply once. “You touched the free connector.”

“I was trying to help.”

“Nobody asked why.”

Leila looks at the cat. He stops talking.

You stand near the coffee stain, which someone has put a wet-floor sign over instead of cleaning. You keep seeing Mum reach for you. Her hand closing on the coat. The patch tearing. You could have held on longer. You know the door was closing. You could still have tried.

Leila finishes wrapping Julian's hand in loose cloth, then comes to you.

“Let me see the elbow.”

“It doesn't matter.”

“Let me see it anyway.”

You push up your sleeve. The dressing has soaked through.

She looks at the cut and goes still in the careful way that means she is trying not to react. Then she takes your arm gently and washes it.

“She was there,” you say.

“I know.”

“You don't. You weren't—”

“I know she was there. I don't know what it was like. Tell me.”

You look at the top of her head. Her hair is going grey near the parting. You haven't noticed that before, though it must have been happening in the same flat you live in.

“She asked about my hair.”

Leila presses her lips together. You try to explain the cable, the panel, the part of Mum's hand you held. None of it comes in the right order. Leila doesn't correct you. When she tapes the clean dressing down, her fingers are shaking.

You say, “Why didn't she call?”

The question has been waiting for eleven months. Now that you know she's alive, it feels harder to ask.

Miso answers from beside Julian's chair.

“Every outside call has a cost. The first ones went to people Adrian chose. After she realised, she stopped trying.”

“Except mine.”

“You don't know who made yours.”

You look at the phone in your hand. Friday, nine forty-seven p.m. The unknown call is listed at two thirteen. Tomorrow's date has returned beside it.

Julian has stopped moving his grey fingers.

“The return line isn't just misdirected,” he says. “It's carrying an instruction. You can't pull her out while that instruction still names her as the receiver.”

“So change the name.”

“To who?”

The room goes quiet.

You hear the idea before you admit it's yours. Your name. Your hand on the cable. Mum walking out. There must be a way to stand where she is until somebody fixes it properly. You could learn enough. You've already learned more today than you thought possible.

Leila looks at you.

“No.”

“I didn't say anything.”

“You didn't have to.”

Imani gets up to leave. You turn towards her, suddenly afraid she has had enough. She lifts her damaged stick.

“I have to be at the rink at ten. My dad's taking me. If I don't sleep now I might as well not go.”

You almost ask how she can think about hockey. Then you see the tape worn smooth where her glove rests, the careful way she avoids putting weight on the damaged blade.

“Can you use another one?”

“I've got an older one.”

“I'm sorry.”

“I know.”

She puts on her coat. At the door she says, “You should come. Not for the hockey. Just get out of here for an hour.”

You look towards the bell cupboard.

“It won't all happen because you sat down,” she says.

After she leaves, Leila makes you eat soup from a paper cup. It is too hot. You drink it anyway, standing near Julian while he uses his good hand to redraw the connector he couldn't reach.

At the bottom of the drawing, beside the receiver slot, he writes one word.

CONSENT.

Then he crosses it out and writes: REMOVAL OF REFUSAL.

You fold the page carefully. This time you know which question to bring back.
