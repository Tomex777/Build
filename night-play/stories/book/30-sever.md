# The Bell in Two Pieces

“Leila. Tell Dennis.”

She looks at the reader, then at Nadia's hand. I see her understand the whole choice before she speaks into the phone.

“Sever the outer circuit. On my count.”

Wren takes one step away from the table.

“Will it hurt?”

“I don't know,” I say.

It is the hardest answer I have given her.

Imani goes to her side. “You can hold my hand if you want.”

Wren nods. The borrowed faces move more slowly across her skin, as if the voices are listening too.

Julian makes sure the second reader is detached from the main line. His good hand is precise. His injured one shakes.

Mum opens two fingers. The cable jerks. I add the last of the chalk to the holding mark and press the point down hard enough to hurt.

“Now,” I say.

Through Leila's phone comes a sharp metal crack.

Nothing happens for half a second. Then every label in the room lifts at once.

The sound is not a scream. It is thousands of unfinished syllables cut short, words losing their ends. A request, an apology, somebody asking where a book has been put. I hear fragments and understand none of them well enough to keep.

Mum's cable goes slack.

Leila pulls the brass loop open with both hands. Nadia cries out. I turn towards her and miss the moment Wren falls.

Imani catches her before she reaches the floor. Wren's face has gone pale and difficult to see. Her receiving card lies on the table. I pick it up and put it in her hand.

“Wren.”

She doesn't answer.

I say it again, not as an order, just as the name I know belongs to the person in front of me.

Her fingers close over the card.

For several seconds, all of us wait. I can hear Mum breathing behind me, uneven and too fast. Julian has put the reader down to kneel beside Imani. Miso stands at Wren's feet, his back arched.

Then Wren opens her eyes.

“Quiet,” she says.

Nobody tries to tell her that's good.

The station begins to change. Without the connections holding its different parts together, the rooms no longer fit. The doorway to Nadia's table becomes a narrow arch. The far switchboard slides towards a wall which wasn't there before.

“We leave,” Julian says.

He doesn't wait for agreement. He lifts the reader, checks that Imani has Wren, and moves towards the stairs. I help Leila support Nadia. Miso leads, stopping whenever the floor changes under his paws.

Voss stands alone beside the master panel. The switches have gone dark. He is pressing one with the end of his thumb, over and over, without enough force to move it.

“Come on,” I tell him.

He doesn't hear me. Or he does and doesn't know what to do with the words.

I go back far enough to take his sleeve.

“We're leaving.”

He looks down at my hand. Then at the empty panel. He comes without resisting.

On the first landing, the wall of water moves inward. I feel cold drops on my face. Imani shifts Wren's arm higher across her shoulders and keeps climbing. Julian goes backwards up two steps so he can guide her feet.

The lower station folds away beneath me.

I don't watch it go. My mother is heavy against my side, and every time she breathes I can feel her ribs move through the coat.

At the top, Dennis stands beside a bell broken into two uneven pieces. The button has rolled under his chair.

He looks at the people coming through the door and counts silently. I see his lips move.

“All out?” he asks.

Leila looks back at the narrowing stair.

“All of us,” she says.

It is accurate. I hear the part it leaves out.
