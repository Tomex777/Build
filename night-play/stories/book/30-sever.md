# The Bell in Two Pieces

“Leila. Tell Dennis.”

She looks at the reader, then at Nadia's hand. You see her understand the whole choice before she speaks into the phone.

“Sever the outer circuit. On my count.”

Wren takes one step away from the table.

“Will it hurt?”

“I don't know,” you say.

It is the hardest answer you have given her.

Imani goes to her side. “You can hold my hand if you want.”

Wren nods. The borrowed faces move more slowly across her skin, as if the voices are listening too.

Julian makes sure the second reader is detached from the main line. His good hand is precise. His injured one shakes.

Mum opens two fingers. The cable jerks. You add the last of the chalk to the holding mark and press the point down hard enough to hurt.

“Now,” you say.

Through Leila's phone comes a sharp metal crack.

Nothing happens for half a second. Then every label in the room lifts at once.

The sound is not a scream. It is thousands of unfinished syllables cut short, words losing their ends. A request, an apology, somebody asking where a book has been put. You hear fragments and understand none of them well enough to keep.

Mum's cable goes slack.

Leila pulls the brass loop open with both hands. Nadia cries out. You turn towards her and miss the moment Wren falls.

Imani catches her before she reaches the floor. Wren's face has gone pale and difficult to see. Her receiving card lies on the table. You pick it up and put it in her hand.

“Wren.”

She doesn't answer.

You say it again, not as an order, just as the name you know belongs to the person in front of you.

Her fingers close over the card.

For several seconds, all of you wait. You can hear Mum breathing behind you, uneven and too fast. Julian has put the reader down to kneel beside Imani. Miso stands at Wren's feet, his back arched.

Then Wren opens her eyes.

“Quiet,” she says.

Nobody tries to tell her that's good.

The station begins to change. Without the connections holding its different parts together, the rooms no longer fit. The doorway to Nadia's table becomes a narrow arch. The far switchboard slides towards a wall which wasn't there before.

“We leave,” Julian says.

He doesn't wait for agreement. He lifts the reader, checks that Imani has Wren, and moves towards the stairs. You help Leila support Nadia. Miso leads, stopping whenever the floor changes under his paws.

Voss stands alone beside the master panel. The switches have gone dark. He is pressing one with the end of his thumb, over and over, without enough force to move it.

“Come on,” you tell him.

He doesn't hear you. Or he does and doesn't know what to do with the words.

You go back far enough to take his sleeve.

“We're leaving.”

He looks down at your hand. Then at the empty panel. He comes without resisting.

On the first landing, the wall of water moves inward. You feel cold drops on your face. Imani shifts Wren's arm higher across her shoulders and keeps climbing. Julian goes backwards up two steps so he can guide her feet.

The lower station folds away beneath you.

You don't watch it go. Your mother is heavy against your side, and every time she breathes you can feel her ribs move through the coat.

At the top, Dennis stands beside a bell broken into two uneven pieces. The button has rolled under his chair.

He looks at the people coming through the door and counts silently. You see his lips move.

“All out?” he asks.

Leila looks back at the narrowing stair.

“All of us,” she says.

It is accurate. You hear the part it leaves out.
