# The Yellow Cupboard

Voss's workshop occupies the second floor of a narrow commercial building. The tenant downstairs repairs office printers. Someone has left three machines in the corridor with PLEASE COLLECT written on them in increasingly large letters.

The side entrance is locked. Imani tries the handle once, then steps aside for Julian. He inspects the lock, looks at the screwdriver in her hand and shakes his head.

“It's a proper lock.”

“What did you think it would be?”

“I was hoping for a bad one.”

Miso noses a loose panel beside the stairs. Behind it, a narrow service gap runs up to the next floor. Too small for a person. Large enough for a cat who has several complaints about dust.

“Don't touch anything yellow until I open the door,” he says.

Then he disappears into the gap.

You wait on the stairs. The building hums with ordinary machinery. Below, a printer starts, feeds one sheet of paper and stops. There is an absurd amount of time to think about being arrested. You imagine explaining the bag of chalk and the manual to a police officer. You imagine Leila arriving to collect you and being more disappointed by your poor planning than the offence.

The lock clicks.

Miso sits inside the door with a grey cobweb across his face. Imani lifts it off. He pretends to dislike the help while leaning into her hand.

The workshop is tidy in a way your mother's never was. Drawers are labelled. Cables hang in even loops. On a bench, an open telephone has each removed screw taped beside its original hole.

At the back stands the yellow cupboard.

Julian checks it against the manual. “Main receiver's in the front. We need the independent reader. Behind.”

Imani goes around the side and finds the thin seam in the false back. She works the screwdriver under it, slowly enough that the wood doesn't split.

You watch the door.

On the wall above the bench is a calendar from the year Eva died. The month hasn't been changed. Someone has crossed out the days after her death and stopped halfway through the page.

A telephone rings in a drawer.

Nobody moves.

It rings again.

Imani keeps working. Julian holds the panel steady with his good hand. You press your fingers against the brass feather in your pocket, needing to feel something that doesn't want an answer.

The drawer opens an inch.

“Sam?” Mum's voice says.

You keep your back to it.

“It's okay. He knows you're here.”

The panel comes loose. Behind it is a small brass box with two sockets and a sliding lever. Julian lifts it with both hands, forgets about his injured one, and nearly drops it. Imani catches the lower edge.

The voice in the drawer laughs softly.

“You keep hurting yourselves.”

“Not Mum,” you say, mostly to yourself.

“I know.”

The answer is so unexpected that you turn.

A girl stands beside the open drawer. She has your face, smaller and badly fitted, as if she has remembered the parts without understanding their distances. While you look at her, the features change into Eva's.

“Better?” she asks.

Miso backs against the door. Julian and Imani stand with the reader between them.

“Who are you?” you ask.

She glances at the calendar.

“That's still the difficult one.”

“Where's Voss?”

“He's coming.”

Downstairs, a door closes. You hear shoes on the first flight.

Imani slides the reader into the equipment bag. “Time to go.”

The girl moves between you and the exit.

“You can't take that. He says it makes people leave.”

“It lets them leave,” you say.

“That's what I said.”

The footsteps stop outside the workshop.

Voss tries the handle. It doesn't move. Miso is standing with a paw pressed against a small chalk mark Julian drew beside the hinge.

“Sam,” Voss calls through the door. “I said you could come and speak to me.”

The girl looks towards him. Her face changes again, into a woman you don't recognise, then an old man with a frightened mouth.

“You shouldn't have come in here,” she says, in several voices at once.

The cupboard doors slam open.

Every cable in the room lifts from its hook.

Imani swings the equipment bag over her shoulder and points at the window. You follow her gaze. A flat roof lies three feet below it, across a narrow gap.

You open the catch. Cold air pours in. The girl reaches for you, stops short of touching your wrist, and looks down at her own hand.

“Will he make you stay?” she asks.

You look at the tightening cables. At Miso holding the door. At the spare key hanging beside the light switch where he could never have reached it without climbing into the claim.

“Not if we leave together.”

The girl stares at you. Behind the door, Voss says a name you can't hear clearly.

Miso cries out.

You have to move.
