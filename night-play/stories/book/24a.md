# A Field With Work to Do

The road back from the witness office passes a doorway which wasn't there when you arrived. Beyond it lie fields under a sky too bright for midnight. Water moves along narrow channels. Someone is repairing a wooden gate, swearing at a peg which refuses to fit.

Wren stops.

“There are voices there.”

Anubis looks through the opening. “People. Different matter.”

A woman carrying a basket comes towards the road. She sets it down beside the gate and helps the man hold the wood level. Neither looks particularly interested in you.

You had expected the dead to be waiting. Here they are busy.

“Field of Reeds?” you ask.

“One way of describing a desired place,” Anubis says. “People make places by living in them too.”

Imani watches the man try the peg again. “Does he want a hammer?”

“He wants the peg he made to fit.”

The answer sounds sufficiently ordinary that she smiles.

Wren takes one step through the doorway. You start to reach for her, then stop. She has a receiving record now. She is not a lost cable that must be kept within reach.

“Can we?” she asks Anubis.

“For a little while. Don't mistake being welcomed for being expected to remain.”

The ground under your boots is warm. After two days of snow and radiators which never quite heat the room, the sensation is so unexpected that you almost sit down.

The woman beside the gate glances at you.

“Living?”

“Yes,” you say.

“Mind the irrigation channel. Looks shallower than it is.”

Then she returns to the gate.

Wren kneels beside the water. Her reflection appears slowly, the chosen face settling into the moving surface. She looks over her shoulder at Imani.

“Is it supposed to do that?”

“Yes.”

“Mine didn't.”

Imani crouches beside her. Their reflections meet in the water. For a while they stay like that without turning it into a lesson.

You walk to the edge of the field with Anubis. The plants are tall enough to move against your sleeves. Farther away, a group of people are carrying baskets towards a house. One pauses to adjust a sandal, falls behind, then hurries to catch the others.

“Did Eva come here?” you ask.

Anubis looks out over the field.

“That isn't information I owe every person who asks.”

“I'm not asking for Voss.”

“I know.”

You let the question go. It is more difficult because he hasn't made it sound foolish.

Near the gate, the peg finally slides into place. The man steps back and tests the wood with both hands. The woman picks up her basket. A task finishes. Nobody asks what must immediately replace it.

Wren comes to stand beside you.

“If I let the voices leave, do I get a place like this?”

Anubis kneels so he is closer to her height.

“You may need to find out what you want after you can hear yourself think.”

“That sounds slow.”

“Sometimes.”

She considers this with obvious dissatisfaction. It is a relief to see her dislike an answer without changing her face to please the person giving it.

Julian waits at the road. He has stayed on the threshold, not because anyone stopped him but because he has discovered a limit he intends to keep. You go back to him before he has to call your name.

“Anything useful?” he asks.

“People doing things,” Imani says.

He looks at her. “Right.”

“No, really. It was useful.”

You understand what she means. The waiting messages have made the dead seem like a single enormous need. Seeing someone argue with a gate puts them back into separate lives. You are not trying to satisfy an ocean of grief. You are trying to stop a machine from making one person's absence into everybody else's obligation.

Wren takes the receiving card out of her pocket. She holds it by the edges, away from the water.

“I want to stay with my own name,” she tells Julian.

He nods. “Then we'll set the reader to recognise the record before it accepts the messages.”

“Can I still say stop?”

“Yes.”

“Even if it makes it harder?”

“Yes.”

She puts the card away. The man at the gate has started walking towards the house, carrying the unused pegs in one hand. He doesn't look back.

At the doorway, you notice a figure sitting in the shade, sewing a tear in a coat. For a second your chest tightens. The angle of the head is familiar. The way the person holds the fabric between two fingers.

Then they look up. Not Mum. A stranger, mildly annoyed by being stared at.

You apologise and keep walking.

Outside, the night returns. Your boots are cold again. Imani checks the time and sends her dad a message before putting her phone away.

You ask Anubis whether he will come with you.

“No.”

You nod before he has to explain the passengers, the road, the jobs which do not stop because you found a worse one. He looks almost relieved.

At the top of the stair, Miso waits beside Julian's trolley. He sees Wren holding the new card and sniffs the edge.

“Good name,” he says.

“I picked it.”

“I know.”

She smiles. The reflection in the stairwell window smiles with her.
