# The First Attempt

The address opens a door in the wall of the exchange room. I know the wall was blank because I helped Julian carry a shelving unit away from it. Now it has a green door with a brass number and a wire-glass window.

Behind the glass, my mother is sitting at a table.

For several seconds I don't move. She looks thinner. Her hair is cut unevenly above her shoulders, with one piece tucked behind an ear. There is a mug near her elbow. She keeps reaching for it and putting it down again without drinking.

I say, “Mum,” but there is no sound. My mouth has done the shape and forgotten the rest.

Imani puts a hand against my back.

I try again.

Mum looks up.

Her chair falls over when she stands. I see her take two steps towards the door, then stop as a cable stretched across the table pulls tight. Her right hand is wrapped around it. The skin across her fingers is red and raw.

“{{firstName}}?”

I nod, then realise how absurd that is. She can see my face. I should say something, tell her I brought help, tell her Leila is waiting, tell her I didn't throw away the blue mug when its handle broke.

What comes out is, “I did my homework.”

Mum puts her free hand over her mouth. For a moment she looks like she might laugh. Then she starts crying, silently, with her eyes still fixed on me.

Julian opens the door just far enough to pass a piece of chalk through. He keeps one foot against the bottom.

“There's a load on the threshold,” he says. “We need something to hold it.”

I kneel and draw the mark. My first circle comes out lopsided. I rub it away with my sleeve and start again. Forty-one seconds in the exchange room wasn't enough. I need longer now. I close the last line and tell the chalk exactly which connection I intend to hold: door, frame, this side, that side.

The green paint goes pale. Julian pushes. The gap widens.

Imani braces her stick against the wall and slides through first. I follow her into a room that smells of dust and hot wiring.

Mum is close enough to touch.

I don't run at her. I think I might break something if I do. I walk carefully around a chair and take her free hand.

It is warm. There is a hard patch on the side of her middle finger where she always held a pen. I press my thumb against it, needing proof more precise than her face.

She pulls me against her shoulder.

“Your hair,” she says.

“Leila cut it.”

“I can tell.”

I make a sound that hurts my throat. Her free arm tightens around me. I would like the next minute to be uncomplicated. I would like at least that much.

The cable jerks. Mum gasps and lets me go.

Julian is standing beside the table, reading the labels on a panel. “We can relieve the hand control. Where's the second reader?”

“Disconnected.”

“Where?”

“Adrian took it out.”

Imani holds the door. “We have six minutes.”

I look at Mum's hand. The cable passes through a loop of brass fitted around her wrist. It isn't tied. The loop has closed into itself.

“Can we cut it?”

“No.”

She says it too quickly, then catches herself.

“Listen. It's carrying people. Messages with the connections still attached. If we cut indiscriminately, the damage travels back to the living end.”

“Then let go.”

“I did. Once.”

The room becomes quiet except for a relay clicking under the table.

Mum points to a row of labels. Three are blank.

“I didn't know who they belonged to until they went blank.”

I want to argue that she couldn't know, that this isn't her fault. But I don't know enough yet to tell her what belongs to her and what doesn't. I hold her free hand instead.

Julian finds a second connector behind the panel. “It's still here. He removed the receiver, not the socket.”

He reaches in.

Miso says, “Wait.”

The room lights go out.

For a second I can hear breathing from everywhere, too many people sharing too little air. Then a child's voice speaks through the wall.

“Dad says you can't take her yet.”

Imani pulls me backwards as the table shifts. One leg sinks through the floor. Mum reaches for me with her free hand, misses, catches my coat.

The seam by the bird patch tears.

Miso leaps onto the panel. His claws scrape metal. Julian is trying to pull the connector free, but his hand has gone rigid. I see the skin on his wrist turning grey.

There is time to reach one of them before the door closes. Mum is standing. Julian isn't moving. Imani's stick has begun to bend against the frame.

I grab Julian's sleeve and pull. He comes away from the panel with a cry. Behind him, the second connector stays where it is.

Mum pushes me towards the door.

“Go.”

“I'm not leaving you.”

“Then you won't be able to come back.”

Imani drags Julian over the threshold. Miso lands beside them. My mother's hand is still on my coat.

I turn and put my hand over hers. One quick pressure. Then she lets go.

The door shuts against my shoulder.

On the blank wall of the exchange room, I leave a streak of blood from my elbow and a white chalk mark that won't close.
