# The First Attempt

The address opens a door in the wall of the exchange room. You know the wall was blank because you helped Julian carry a shelving unit away from it. Now it has a green door with a brass number and a wire-glass window.

Behind the glass, your mother is sitting at a table.

For several seconds you don't move. She looks thinner. Her hair is cut unevenly above her shoulders, with one piece tucked behind an ear. There is a mug near her elbow. She keeps reaching for it and putting it down again without drinking.

You say, “Mum,” but there is no sound. Your mouth has done the shape and forgotten the rest.

Imani puts a hand against your back.

You try again.

Mum looks up.

Her chair falls over when she stands. You see her take two steps towards the door, then stop as a cable stretched across the table pulls tight. Her right hand is wrapped around it. The skin across her fingers is red and raw.

“Sam?”

You nod, then realise how absurd that is. She can see your face. You should say something, tell her you brought help, tell her Leila is waiting, tell her you didn't throw away the blue mug when its handle broke.

What comes out is, “I did my homework.”

Mum puts her free hand over her mouth. For a moment she looks like she might laugh. Then she starts crying, silently, with her eyes still fixed on you.

Julian opens the door just far enough to pass a piece of chalk through. He keeps one foot against the bottom.

“There's a load on the threshold,” he says. “We need something to hold it.”

You kneel and draw the mark. Your first circle comes out lopsided. You rub it away with your sleeve and start again. Forty-one seconds in the exchange room wasn't enough. You need longer now. You close the last line and tell the chalk exactly which connection you intend to hold: door, frame, this side, that side.

The green paint goes pale. Julian pushes. The gap widens.

Imani braces her stick against the wall and slides through first. You follow her into a room that smells of dust and hot wiring.

Mum is close enough to touch.

You don't run at her. You think you might break something if you do. You walk carefully around a chair and take her free hand.

It is warm. There is a hard patch on the side of her middle finger where she always held a pen. You press your thumb against it, needing proof more precise than her face.

She pulls you against her shoulder.

“Your hair,” she says.

“Leila cut it.”

“I can tell.”

You make a sound that hurts your throat. Her free arm tightens around you. You would like the next minute to be uncomplicated. You would like at least that much.

The cable jerks. Mum gasps and lets you go.

Julian is standing beside the table, reading the labels on a panel. “We can relieve the hand control. Where's the second reader?”

“Disconnected.”

“Where?”

“Adrian took it out.”

Imani holds the door. “We have six minutes.”

You look at Mum's hand. The cable passes through a loop of brass fitted around her wrist. It isn't tied. The loop has closed into itself.

“Can we cut it?”

“No.”

She says it too quickly, then catches herself.

“Listen. It's carrying people. Messages with the connections still attached. If we cut indiscriminately, the damage travels back to the living end.”

“Then let go.”

“I did. Once.”

The room becomes quiet except for a relay clicking under the table.

Mum points to a row of labels. Three are blank.

“I didn't know who they belonged to until they went blank.”

You want to argue that she couldn't know, that this isn't her fault. But you don't know enough yet to tell her what belongs to her and what doesn't. You hold her free hand instead.

Julian finds a second connector behind the panel. “It's still here. He removed the receiver, not the socket.”

He reaches in.

Miso says, “Wait.”

The room lights go out.

For a second you can hear breathing from everywhere, too many people sharing too little air. Then a child's voice speaks through the wall.

“Dad says you can't take her yet.”

Imani pulls you backwards as the table shifts. One leg sinks through the floor. Mum reaches for you with her free hand, misses, catches your coat.

The seam by the bird patch tears.

Miso leaps onto the panel. His claws scrape metal. Julian is trying to pull the connector free, but his hand has gone rigid. You see the skin on his wrist turning grey.

There is time to reach one of them before the door closes. Mum is standing. Julian isn't moving. Imani's stick has begun to bend against the frame.

You grab Julian's sleeve and pull. He comes away from the panel with a cry. Behind him, the second connector stays where it is.

Mum pushes you towards the door.

“Go.”

“I'm not leaving you.”

“Then you won't be able to come back.”

Imani drags Julian over the threshold. Miso lands beside them. Your mother's hand is still on your coat.

You turn and put your hand over hers. One quick pressure. Then she lets go.

The door shuts against your shoulder.

On the blank wall of the exchange room, you leave a streak of blood from your elbow and a white chalk mark that won't close.
