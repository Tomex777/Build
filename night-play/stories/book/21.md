# The Gap Between Buildings

Imani goes through the window first. She drops onto the roof, turns and holds out both hands for the equipment bag. Julian passes it down, then climbs after it with his injured hand tucked against his chest.

Miso is still at the door.

The cables have reached the floor. One moves over your boot with the deliberate pressure of a finger testing fabric. You lift your foot. It follows.

You take the chalk from your pocket and draw the first release mark on the desk. The line breaks where the surface is covered in varnish. You rub it away and try on the exposed wood beneath a scratch.

The girl's face changes as she watches.

“You know that one.”

“I'm trying to.”

“He doesn't like that one.”

Miso makes another small sound. You don't look at him. If you look at him, you'll hurry the last line and lose it.

“Can you hold this end?” you ask the girl.

She takes a step back.

“Not for him,” you say. “For the cat. Only until he moves. Then you stop.”

She looks at the mark, then at her own hands. The cables around your boots loosen.

“Only until he moves,” she repeats.

“Yes.”

She puts two fingers inside the outer circle. The chalk glows a dull white. You feel a resistance at the edge of the mark, something trying to keep its old shape.

Miso lifts his paw from the door.

The handle turns. You scoop him up, feel his claws close hard around your sleeve, and run for the window.

Voss opens the door behind you.

“Eva.”

The girl pulls her hand out of the chalk circle. You are already on the sill. She looks towards him, then towards the gap.

You don't tell her what to do. You drop.

The roof knocks the air out of your chest. Imani catches your coat, preventing you from sliding into a shallow pool beside the gutter. Miso scrambles out of your arms and crouches against a vent.

Above you, Voss leans out of the window.

“That isn't going to hold the line by itself,” he calls. “You need the calibration I gave Nadia.”

Julian is checking the brass reader. “We have it.”

“No,” Voss says, and for the first time he sounds genuinely alarmed. “You have the original. The load changed.”

The girl steps onto the sill beside him.

“Come inside,” he says.

She looks at the snow collecting on the roof.

“Is that cold?”

“Come inside.”

She lowers herself over the edge. For a moment her shape stretches too far, hand still on the sill while her feet touch the roof. Then she lets go and stands beside you, small and barefoot.

Voss says her borrowed name again. She covers her ears.

The roof door is locked from the other side. Imani goes at it with the screwdriver. Julian holds the bag. You keep your body between the girl and the window, although you're not sure either of you would survive Voss deciding to pull a connection instead of calling.

Then Leila opens the roof door.

All of you stare at her.

“Stairs,” she says. “Building has them. Move.”

She has Dennis behind her. He looks at the workshop window, the girl and the equipment bag, then decides not to ask until everyone is inside.

Downstairs, the printer-repair tenant is standing by his front door with folded arms.

“Do I need to call the police?”

“Yes,” Leila says. “Tell them there's an electrical hazard upstairs. Stay out until someone checks it.”

She doesn't try to give him the rest. You are learning that honesty can be a matter of answering the question someone actually needs answered.

On the street, the girl stops under a lamp. Snow passes through her shoulder, then begins to settle. She holds one flake on the back of her hand and watches it melt.

“It doesn't stay,” she says.

“No.”

“Good.”

Miso approaches her slowly. His collar is twisted to one side. She crouches and offers him her empty hand. He sniffs it without touching.

“What do I call you?” you ask.

The girl looks across the road. On the pavement near a hedge, a small brown bird hops away from the lights.

“What's that?”

“I don't know. A sparrow?”

Imani squints. “Maybe a wren.”

The girl considers both names with obvious seriousness.

“Wren.”

“You don't have to pick now.”

“I want that one.”

“Okay. Wren.”

Her face changes. Eva's features loosen. For a moment she looks unfinished, a person drawn before the lines have been rubbed clean. Then she settles into a face you have never seen before.

Dennis opens the car door. “Can she wear a seat belt?”

Wren looks at it, then at you.

“We can find out,” you say.

In the back seat, Imani passes her a spare pair of gloves. Wren puts both on the same hand, studies the result and waits for someone to explain.

Nobody laughs. Imani takes one off and shows her how.
