Endless third-party texture notice

Planetary texture maps are sourced from Solar System Scope:
https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/

Solar System Scope states that the texture pack is based on NASA elevation and imagery data
and is distributed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license (CC BY 4.0).

Attribution: Solar System Scope.
License: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

Endless uses these maps as visualization assets. Astronomical metadata and simulations remain
separate from texture imagery.

Close-approach lunar material:
- Lunar close albedo is a 4096×2048 Solar System Scope Moon map redistributed by
  galaxy-explorer; Solar System Scope attribution and CC BY 4.0 terms apply.
- Lunar normal detail is derived from the NASA Scientific Visualization Studio
  CGI Moon Kit / Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter LOLA displacement data. NASA/GSFC,
  Ernie Wright (USRA), Noah Petro (NASA/GSFC), and the LOLA team are credited.

Close-approach Mars normal detail is derived from the USGS Astrogeology MGS MOLA
global elevation product, credited to the MOLA Team and NASA GSFC.


Measured planetary surface data:
- NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio CGI Moon Kit (ID 4720): 2025 2K
  LRO/LROC natural-color lunar texture and LRO/LOLA `ldem_16_uint.tif`
  elevation raster. Credited to NASA/GSFC, Ernie Wright, Noah Petro, the
  LROC and LOLA teams. https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720/
- NASA Mars Global Surveyor/Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter MEGDR
  `megt90n000eb.img` topography, courtesy NASA GSFC MOLA science team
  and PDS Geosciences Node.
  https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/missions/mgs/megdr.html
- Raster DEMs are cropped/resampled to compact Tycho (-43.31, 348.64 E)
  and Olympus Mons (18.65 N, 226.2 E) landing patches. Native source
  resolution is 16 pixels/degree. Bilinear interpolation does not create
  additional measured geological detail; subpixel visual detail remains
  illustrative rather than measured.
- Approximate planetary orbital elements and analytical J2000 solution:
  NASA/JPL Solar System Dynamics, Standish/Williams Table 1 (1800-2050).
  https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html
  Present renderer magnifies planet sizes and compresses planet distances
  for navigation, despite improving Keplerian phase/tilt/eccentricity.

Blue Marble Next Generation photographic Earth imagery:
- Endless uses a NASA Earth Observatory Blue Marble July composite
  (source world.200407.3x5400x2700.jpg; NASA/GSFC, Reto Stöckli).
  Delivered in a genuine downsampled 4096x2048 image by physics-lab:
  https://github.com/MaxwellLee/physics-lab/blob/main/assets/textures/SOURCES.md
  NASA original: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-map/
  No clouds, shadows, and artificial lighting are baked into this day albedo.

NASA Trek photographic landings (when imagery server is available during CI):
- Moon: LRO Wide Angle Camera (WAC) Global Mosaic 303ppd v02,
  NASA Moon Trek (NASA/GSFC, ASU LROC team), at Tycho Crater -43.31 latitude,
  348.64 degrees east longitude.
  https://trek.nasa.gov/tiles/apidoc/trekAPI.html?body=moon
- Mars: Viking MDIM 2.1 Color Mosaic global 232 m,
  NASA Mars Trek (NASA/JPL/USGS), near Olympus Mons 18.65 latitude,
  226.2 degrees east longitude.
  https://trek.nasa.gov/tiles/apidoc/trekAPI.html?body=mars
- Product is a georeferenced ~81.6 km by ~81.6 km NASA orbital photo mosaic,
  matched with MOLA and LOLA DEM coordinates. The orbital images are NOT
  meter-scale rover photography or ground-truth boulder textures.
- The app works entirely offline after installation. If the NASA Trek service
  is unavailable during packaging, the app falls back to geologic materials
  generated procedurally above the measured DEM and indicates that fallback
  in CI logs. The product should not be marketed as photographic without
  checking inclusion of these resources.

Sub-10-cm grain reference (optional, generated only when NASA photos can be fetched):
- Mars reference: NASA/JPL-Caltech/Cornell/ASU, Opportunity Pancam soil image
  PIA14533 from Endeavour crater rim, 2011.
  https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia14533-on-different-ground-soil-on-endeavour-rim/
- Moon reference: NASA/JSC Apollo 11 photograph AS11-40-5876, undisturbed soil
  at Tranquility Base (via Wikimedia Commons, public domain).
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:AS11-40-5876_Undisturbed_Lunar_Soil.png
- These are normalised, high-pass, tiny seamless grain patterns, *not* measured
  images of Olympus Mons or Tycho crater. Large-scale geographical photos
  continue to use locally registered NASA Trek mosaics.
- Each generated WebP is 256x256; raw photo is not bundled. If fetch fails,
  the app uses its built-in deterministic regolith atlas and remains offline.


Expanded exploratory worlds:
- Mercury: MESSENGER Mercury Dual Imaging System enhanced-color global
  basemap (~665 m source scale) served by NASA Mercury Trek. Selected map
  center approximately Caloris Basin (30.5 N, 162.7 E).
  https://trek.nasa.gov/tiles/apidoc/trekAPI.html?body=mercury
- Ceres: NASA Dawn Framing Camera HAMO enhanced color/shaded global mosaic,
  serviced by NASA Ceres Trek; centered near Occator (20 N, 239 E).
  https://trek.nasa.gov/tiles/apidoc/trekAPI.html?body=ceres
- Those optional mission mosaics register ORBITAL REFLECTANCE to geographic
  map coordinates. They do NOT provide measured landing-site geometry:
  Mercury/Ceres, Europa and Titan terrain is presently illustrative 3D
  procedural geology and should be labelled SIMULATED.
- Europa's ice fractures, ridges, and likely ocean are modeled from NASA
  Galileo/Europa Clipper descriptions; Titan's dunes and atmospheric haze
  from NASA Cassini/Huygens observations. Both satellite orbits are simplified
  parent-relative motions, NOT historical JPL ephemerides.
  https://europa.nasa.gov/mission/faq/
  https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/moons/titan/facts/
- NASA/JPL-Caltech, ASU and the mission instrument teams are credited for
  the photographic sources. If optional tile fetching fails, the new worlds
  remain navigable but show only explicitly illustrative materials.

Optional NASA Olympus Mons local photographic detail:
- NASA/JPL/USGS Mars Trek WMTS layer ID olympus_mons.eq, centered on
  Olympus Mons (18.65 N, -133.8 E), used as a separate luminance
  detail source IF a complete tile mosaic is available in CI.
  https://trek.nasa.gov/tiles/apidoc/trekAPI.html?body=mars
- The verified Viking MDIM 2.1 color is always the base orbital
